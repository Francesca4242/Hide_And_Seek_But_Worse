Form 27B, formally the Hiding Declaration (Domestic), is the standard document every hider is required to file before a round of hide and seek may commence under Departmental rules. It requests, among other things, the hider's intended concealment location and an Estimated Time of Discovery — a figure the Department has never once used for anything, but insists on collecting regardless.

Completed forms are held on file for the duration of the round and are consulted only twice: once by the seeker, in the form of a heavily redacted Freedom of Information response, and once, unpredictably, by a roaming Compliance Audit — a short quiz in which the hider is asked to recall exactly what they wrote. Hiders who answer correctly are awarded Compliance Points and, per longstanding custom, a Pre-Approved Alibi Chit. Hiders who answer incorrectly are quietly noted as "inconsistent" and their position logged accordingly.

Whether anyone has ever been meaningfully helped or harmed by Form 27B remains, like most things at the Department, a matter of ongoing review.
