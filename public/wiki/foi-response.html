Freedom of Information (Hide and Seek) Response

From the Departmental Archive — the free-ish encyclopedia

The Freedom of Information (Hide and Seek) response, commonly the FOI response, is the document issued to a seeker in place of a plain list of hiders' names and locations. The Department has long held that a plain list would be far too useful, and therefore withholds one as a matter of policy.

In its place, the seeker receives each hider's declared information from Form 27B, with names partially blacked out and locations quoted verbatim, unverified, and explicitly marked "non-binding." A hider's true identity is disclosed only once they have been formally located, at which point the redaction is lifted with what Departmental style guides describe as "quiet satisfaction."

Critics note that the FOI response frequently tells the seeker less than they could have learned simply by looking around the room. The Department considers this a feature.
